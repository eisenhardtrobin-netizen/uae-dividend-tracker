How UAE Stock Dividends Work: A Complete Beginner's Guide

Updated July 2026 · 8 min read

If you invest in stocks listed on the Dubai Financial Market (DFM) or Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange (ADX), dividends can be a significant source of returns. But the mechanics of UAE dividends differ slightly from other markets. This guide explains everything you need to know.

What is a Dividend?

A dividend is a portion of a company's profits distributed to shareholders. In the UAE, most listed companies pay dividends annually (once a year), though an increasing number now pay semi-annually or quarterly.

Dividend amounts are expressed as AED per share. For example, if First Abu Dhabi Bank (FAB) declares a dividend of AED 0.80 per share and you own 1,000 shares, you'll receive AED 800.

Key Dates You Must Know

There are three critical dates for every dividend payment:

DateWhat It Means
Buy-By Date (Record Date minus settlement)The last day you can BUY the stock and still qualify for the dividend. On DFM/ADX, settlement is T+2, so you must buy at least 2 trading days before the record date.
Ex-Dividend DateThe first day the stock trades WITHOUT the dividend. If you buy on or after this date, you do NOT get the upcoming dividend.
Payment DateThe day the dividend cash lands in your brokerage account.
Key takeaway: You must own the stock BEFORE the ex-dividend date to receive the payment. The buy-by date is typically 1 business day before the ex-date on DFM/ADX (due to T+2 settlement).

How T+2 Settlement Works

When you buy a stock on DFM or ADX, the trade settles in 2 business days (T+2). This means:

  • If the ex-dividend date is Wednesday, you must buy by Monday (2 business days earlier)
  • If you buy on Tuesday (1 day before ex), settlement happens Thursday — after the record date — so you miss the dividend

This is why the "buy-by date" is so important. It accounts for settlement automatically.

UAE Dividend Tax

One of the biggest advantages of UAE stock investing: there is no dividend tax in the UAE. You receive the full declared amount with zero withholding. This makes UAE dividend stocks particularly attractive compared to markets that withhold 15-30% tax on dividends.

Annual vs. Quarterly Dividends

Traditionally, most UAE stocks paid dividends once per year (after the AGM in Q1). However, since 2025, several major companies have switched to quarterly payouts:

  • ADNOC Distribution — Quarterly (since Q1 2026)
  • ADNOC Gas — Quarterly (since Q3 2025)
  • ADNOC Drilling — Quarterly
  • TAQA — Quarterly
  • ADNOC L&S — Quarterly

Quarterly dividends mean more frequent buy-by deadlines to track — but also more regular income if you hold these stocks.

How to Build a UAE Dividend Portfolio

  1. Open a brokerage account with access to DFM and ADX (e.g., ADSS, Interactive Brokers, or a local UAE broker)
  2. Research dividend yields — compare the annual dividend to the stock price. A stock paying AED 0.80 at a price of AED 13.50 has a yield of ~5.9%
  3. Check the dividend calendar — know when each stock's buy-by date is so you can position in advance
  4. Diversify across sectors — UAE markets offer high-yield stocks in banking, energy, telecom, and real estate
  5. Reinvest or take income — decide whether to reinvest dividends for compounding or use them as passive income

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Buying on the ex-date — you won't get the dividend. Always check the buy-by date.
  • Ignoring settlement — remember T+2. Buying the day before the ex-date is too late.
  • Chasing yield only — a high yield can indicate a falling stock price. Check fundamentals too.
  • Not tracking frequency changes — several ADNOC companies switched from annual/semi-annual to quarterly in 2025-2026. Outdated data means missed deadlines.

Conclusion

UAE stock dividends offer a compelling income opportunity — tax-free, with yields among the highest in the Gulf. The key is knowing your dates: when to buy, when the stock goes ex-dividend, and when you get paid.
